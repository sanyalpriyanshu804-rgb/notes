# **PART 1: ELECTROSTATIC POTENTIAL ENERGY (U)**

### **1. Concept Building**
**The "Why" and "What":** Potential energy is not a property of a single charge but of a **system**. It exists because the electrostatic force is **conservative**. In any conservative field (like gravity or a spring), the work done against the field is stored as potential energy. 

**Intuition:** Imagine trying to bring a positive charge $+q_2$ near a fixed positive charge $+q_1$. As you push it from infinity, it feels a repulsive force. You must do work to move it closer. This work doesn't disappear; it is stored in the "electric field" of the system, ready to be converted into kinetic energy the moment you release the charge.

### **2. Detailed Theory**
*   **Fundamental Definition:** The change in potential energy ($\Delta U$) is defined as the negative of the work done by internal conservative (electrostatic) forces ($W_{int}$).
    $$\Delta U = U_f - U_i = -W_{elec}$$
*   **Work Done by External Agent:** If a charge is moved **slowly** (without change in kinetic energy), the work done by the external agent ($W_{ext}$) equals the change in potential energy.
# derivation

    $$W_{ext} = \Delta U \text{ (when } \Delta KE = 0)$$
*   **Potential Energy of Two Point Charges:**
    $$U = \frac{k q_1 q_2}{r}$$
    *   **Assumption:** Potential energy is assumed to be zero at infinity ($r = \infty$).
    *   **Sign Convention:** Always put charges with their signs. If $U$ is positive, the system is repulsive; if $U$ is negative, the system is attractive.
*   **System of $n$ Charges:** The total potential energy is the sum of interaction energies of all possible pairs ($^nC_2$ pairs).
    *   For 3 charges: $U_{sys} = \frac{k q_1 q_2}{r_{12}} + \frac{k q_1 q_3}{r_{13}} + \frac{k q_2 q_3}{r_{23}}$.

#### **“PHYSICS INTUITION”**
*   **Energy Interpretation:** A negative potential energy means you would have to *supply* energy to pull the charges to infinity (the system is "bound").
*   **Equilibrium:** Systems naturally try to move toward states of **minimum potential energy**.

# **PART 2: ELECTRIC POTENTIAL (V)**

### **1. Concept Building**
**Analogy:** If Electric Potential Energy is like "Total Cost," Electric Potential is like "Price per Unit." 
**Why it exists:** Potential is a **property of a point in space** created by a source charge, independent of the test charge placed there. It is a scalar tool that allows us to solve complex energy problems without using vectors (Electric Field).

### **2. Detailed Theory**
*   **Definition:** Potential at a point is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to that point.
    $$V = \frac{U}{q}$$
*   **Relation between $\vec{E}$ and $V$:** 
    $$\Delta V = V_f - V_i = -\int \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r}$$
# derivation

    *   **Physical Interpretation:** The negative sign indicates that the electric field points in the direction of **decreasing potential**.
*   **Potential due to a Point Charge:** $V = \frac{kq}{r}$.

#### **3. Potential for Standard Geometries**
For a charge Q at a distance r, V= kQ/r
Finite charged rod(length L): V = (kQ/L) x ln[(a+L)/a]

1.  **Ring (at Center):** $V = \frac{kQ}{R}$ (All charge is at distance $R$).
2.  **Ring (on Axis):** $V = \frac{kQ}{\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}}$.

Uniformly charged disc(Axial point)-
V = (σ / 2ε₀) [√(R² + x²) − x]   OR   V = (σ / 2ε₀) (1 − cosθ) √(R² + x²)

3.  **Thin Spherical Shell (Hollow Sphere):** 
    *   **Inside ($r < R$):** $V = \frac{kQ}{R}$ (**Constant!**).
    *   **Outside ($r \geq R$):** $V = \frac{kQ}{r}$.
4.  **Solid Non-Conducting Sphere:**
    *   **Inside ($r < R$):** $V = \frac{kQ}{2R^3}(3R^2 - r^2)$ (The "Chuudail Formula").
    *   **At Center ($r = 0$):** $V_c = 1.5 \times V_{surface} = \frac{3kQ}{2R}$.

# **PART 3: ELECTRIC DIPOLE**

### **1. Detailed Theory**
*   **Definition:** A pair of equal and opposite charges ($+q$ and $-q$) separated by a very small distance $d$.
*   **Dipole Moment ($\vec{p}$):** A vector quantity.
    $$\vec{p} = q \cdot \vec{d}$$
    *   **Direction:** From negative charge to positive charge.
*   **Electric Field due to Short Dipole:**
    *   **Axial Point:** $\vec{E}_{axial} = \frac{2k\vec{p}}{r^3}$ (Direction of $\vec{E}$ is same as $\vec{p}$).
    *   **Equatorial Point:** $\vec{E}_{equi} = -\frac{k\vec{p}}{r^3}$ (Direction of $\vec{E}$ is opposite to $\vec{p}$).
    *   **General Point ($\theta$):** E_net = (k p / r^3) * sqrt(1 + 3 cos^2(theta)).
*   **Potential due to Dipole:** $V = \frac{kp\cos\theta}{r^2}$.
# potential due to dipole at any general point: V = k(vec_p) dot (vec_r)/r^3

### **2. Dipole in Uniform External Field ($\vec{E}_{ext}$)**
*   **Net Force:** $F_{net} = 0$ (in uniform field).
*   **Torque:** $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$.
*   **Potential Energy:** $U = -\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E} = -pE\cos\theta$.
*   **Work Done in Rotating a Dipole:** $W_{ext} = pE(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$.

#### **“PHYSICS INTUITION”**
*   **Stable Equilibrium:** When $\vec{p}$ is parallel to $\vec{E}$ ($\theta = 0^\circ$). $U$ is minimum and $\tau = 0$.
*   **Unstable Equilibrium:** When $\vec{p}$ is anti-parallel to $\vec{E}$ ($\theta = 180^\circ$). $U$ is maximum and $\tau = 0$.
*   **Oscillation:** If a dipole in stable equilibrium is displaced by a small angle $\theta$, it performs **Angular SHM**.
    $$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{pE}}$$

# **PART 4: ELECTROSTATICS OF CONDUCTORS**

1.  **Electric Field Inside:** $\vec{E} = 0$ where material exists.
2.  **Net Charge:** Any excess charge resides only on the **outer surface**.
3.  **Potential:** The entire conductor (including the interior) is at the same potential (Equipotential).
4.  **Field Lines:** Always hit or leave the surface **perpendicularly**.
5.  **Surface Charge Density ($\sigma$):** $\sigma \propto \frac{1}{R}$ (Charge accumulates more at sharp points/small radius of curvature).

# **PART 5: SELF-POTENTIAL ENERGY ($U_{self}$)**

### **Concept Building**
**The "Why" and "What":** 
Self-potential energy is the energy required to **assemble a charged system** from scratch by bringing infinitesimal charge elements from infinity to their final configuration. 

**Intuition:** 
Imagine you are building a charged solid sphere. When you bring the first tiny bit of charge ($dq$) from infinity, there is no work done because there is no field to oppose you. However, when you bring the second bit of charge, the first one repels it. By the time you are adding the last bit of charge to a large sphere, you are fighting against the massive repulsion of all the charge already there. This total work done is stored as the "self-energy" of that object.

**What problem it solves:**
It helps us calculate the total energy stored in a field and determine the work required when the **geometry** (like the radius) of a charged body changes.

### **Detailed Theory and Formulas**

#### **A. Point Charge**
*   **Formula:** $U_{self} = 0$.
*   **Reasoning:** A single point charge has no internal parts to repel each other. Since you don't do work to place the "first" bit of charge in an empty universe, its self-energy is considered zero in standard JEE problems.

#### **B. Hollow Spherical Shell (or Conducting Sphere)**
*   **Formula:** $U_{self} = \frac{kQ^2}{2R}$ or $\frac{Q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0 R}$.
*   **Assumptions:** Charge $Q$ is distributed uniformly on the surface of radius $R$.
*   **Interpretation:** This represents the energy stored in the electric field existing from $r = R$ to $r = \infty$.

#### **C. Solid Non-Conducting Sphere**
*   **Formula:** $U_{self} = \frac{3}{5} \frac{kQ^2}{R}$.
*   **Derivation Logic:** This requires assembling charge layer by layer from the center outward. Because charge is also packed *inside* the volume (closer together), the energy is higher than that of a hollow shell.

*   **Unit Analysis:** Both formulas result in units of Joules ($k \cdot Q^2 / r \Rightarrow [Nm^2/C^2] \cdot [C^2] / [m] = Nm = J$).

### **Deep Physics Intuition**
*   **Energy Comparison:** Notice that $U_{solid} (0.6 \frac{kQ^2}{R}) > U_{shell} (0.5 \frac{kQ^2}{R})$. This is because, in a solid sphere, charges are forced to be closer to the center, leading to higher repulsive potential energy than if they were all moved to the surface.
*   **Work and Expansion:** If a charged soap bubble (shell) expands from radius $R_1$ to $R_2$, its self-energy decreases. The **work done by the electric field** is $U_i - U_f$. 
*   **Internal vs. External Work:** If you (the external agent) change the radius of a shell, the work you do is $W_{ext} = \Delta U_{self} = U_{final} - U_{initial}$.

*   **Rule of Thumb:** Only include $U_{self}$ if the **size or shape** of the charged body is changing (e.g., a drop splitting into smaller droplets or a bubble expanding).

#### **Standard Pattern: The "N-Drops" Problem**
When $N$ identical small droplets (each with charge $q$, radius $r$) merge to form a big drop:
1.  **Charge Conservation:** $Q = Nq$.
2.  **Volume Conservation:** $\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = N(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3) \Rightarrow R = N^{1/3}r$.
3.  **Energy Change:** Calculate $U_{initial} = N \times (\text{self-energy of one small drop})$ and $U_{final} = \text{self-energy of the big drop}$. The energy released/absorbed is the difference.


### **Advanced Concept: Interaction vs. Self-Energy**
Total Energy of a system = $\sum U_{self} + \sum U_{interaction}$.
*   **Interaction Energy:** Energy due to the relative positions of different bodies ($kQ_1Q_2/r$).
*   **Self-Energy:** Energy due to the assembly of a single body ($3/5 kQ^2/R$).

# **PART 6: JEE PROBLEM SOLVING TECHNIQUES**

*   **Mechanical Energy Conservation:** In problems involving moving charges (minimum separation, speed at center), always use $KE_i + U_i = KE_f + U_f$.
*   **The "Setting" Method for Shells:** When multiple shells are given, start from the innermost charge and place opposite charges on the facing surfaces to ensure $E_{material} = 0$.
*   **Earthing:** When a conductor is earthed, its **Potential ($V$) becomes zero**, not its charge.
*   **Parallel Plate Charge Distribution:** For a system of parallel plates, the charge on the outermost surfaces is always half the total sum of all charges: $Q_{outer} = \frac{\sum Q_i}{2}$.
